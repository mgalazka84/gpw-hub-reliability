# Computational Statistics manuscript files

This folder contains the manuscript prepared for a new submission to
Computational Statistics. It is separate from the earlier CSDA assessment.

- `manuscript_practical_value.pdf`: complete paper with author information.
- `manuscript_practical_value_anonymized.pdf`: paper without author details.
- `Supplementary_analyses.pdf`: Online Resource 1.
- `Computational_Statistics_LaTeX.zip`: complete main-paper source in a flat directory.
- `Computational_Statistics_Supplement_LaTeX.zip`: complete supplementary source.
- `Highlights.txt`: optional concise points if requested by the submission form.

The source archives have been compiled in fresh directories and checked against
the supplied PDFs. The primary implementation and immutable numerical-results
snapshot are linked from the paper and `../power_revision/README.md`.

Cover letters and private colleague-review correspondence are supplied directly
to the authors, not published here. Files in this repository are materials for
submission; their presence does not mean that a journal submission has been sent.

The current files include the complete 100-split sensitivity check. The reported
questionnaire split is favourable: the three shared hub members recur in 16/100
extra splits. All 20 application methods are displayed without retrospective
primary/secondary ranking. PROVENANCE_AUDIT.txt explains the public record.

The sequence audit reproduces all 100 archived FS-T64 outcomes and identifies the first failed contrasts. The supplement reports measured questionnaire stage timings, and the paper explicitly reports losses relative to E64 in 39/100 splits.

The validity proposition explicitly includes single-matching P evidence after independent selection. Timing scope distinguishes the first three stages shared by all 15 split variants from FS-T64 projection stages. Minor projection plus exact enumeration takes 2.44 ms (sum of stage medians), about 0.13% of shared evidence evaluation; optional strong LP adds 0.57 s without changing the questionnaire conclusions.

The split comparison separates preservation of baseline conclusions from stronger shared-hub certification: H-E64 retains all E64 bounds and guaranteed hub members in 94/100 splits, versus 61/100 for FS-T64; the three shared hubs recur in 6/100 versus 16/100. With calibration held at E64, FS-E64 retains the baseline in 49/100 and certifies the three members in 10/100. These descriptive results do not establish uniform superiority of one procedure.
